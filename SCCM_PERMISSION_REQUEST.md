**To:** iculiberation@sccm.org
**Subject:** Permission and licensing enquiry — commercial nursing education platform referencing the ICU Liberation Bundle (A–F)

Dear ICU Liberation team,

My name is [Full name], [role] of **ICU Nurse Knowledge Map**, an independently developed, commercial online education platform for adult ICU nurses ([country; planned launch region(s)]). The platform will offer subscription-based topic modules, clinical-skills content, a medication reference, interactive patient simulations and an exam question bank. It is not affiliated with SCCM or any other organisation.

We would like to teach the ICU Liberation Bundle (A–F) as part of our curriculum — pain assessment, sedation and agitation assessment, delirium prevention and screening, spontaneous awakening and breathing trials, early mobility and family engagement — and we want to do this properly and with your permission where required. Our content will be written independently by our authors and reviewed by clinicians, and will cite the underlying peer-reviewed evidence, including the 2018 PADIS guidelines.

We would be grateful for your guidance on the following.

1. **Commercial use of the ABCDEF Bundle.** May a paid platform describe and teach the bundle's elements in original wording, citing SCCM as the source? Are there conditions on how the bundle is named or described?
2. **Figures and graphics.** May we reproduce, or must we avoid reproducing, any ICU Liberation figures, infographics, flowcharts or illustrations? If permitted, what is the process? (Our default is to create our own original diagrams.)
3. **Assessment tools and resources.** Are the tools and resources in the ICU Liberation resource library (for example worksheets, checklists, pocket cards, training slides) available for use within a paid platform? Please tell us which items are SCCM-owned and which belong to third parties (for example, tool developers), so we can approach them separately.
4. **Adaptation into interactive education.** May we adapt the bundle's concepts into interactive formats — branching patient simulations, quizzes and case studies — written in our own words? Do you require review or approval of such adaptations?
5. **Attribution.** What acknowledgement wording and placement do you require (including any logo use, which we would not use without your explicit consent)? Is any statement of non-endorsement required?
6. **Licensing fees.** Is there a licence fee, royalty or tiered pricing for commercial educational use? If so, how is it calculated (for example by revenue, subscriber numbers or content volume)?
7. **Written permission procedure.** What is your process and expected timeline for written permission, and what information do you need from us (content samples, pricing model, geographic scope, languages)?
8. **Translation.** We plan to offer Arabic content in the future. Does any permission extend to translated or localised versions, or is that a separate request?
9. **Updates.** How will we be notified of changes to the bundle or to licence terms, and how long may previously licensed content remain in use after an update?

Until we hear from you we will not reproduce any SCCM-protected materials, and we will limit ourselves to citing published evidence and linking to SCCM's own resources.

I would be happy to provide an outline of the planned content, our pricing model or a demonstration, or to arrange a call.

Thank you for your time and for the work SCCM does for critically ill patients.
