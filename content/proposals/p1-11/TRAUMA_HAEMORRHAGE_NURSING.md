# Major trauma: bleeding, coagulopathy and ICU handover
> **UNPUBLISHED PROPOSAL. AI-authored; not clinically reviewed.** Not a trauma resuscitation algorithm or medication/dose chart. Outside original 40 topics, learner website and case bank.

## Scope and learning objectives
A nursing-level module for an injured adult with suspected major haemorrhage, including coexisting traumatic brain injury (TBI) as a key exception. Learners will: (1) identify life-threatening bleeding and shock deterioration; (2) escalate and coordinate bleeding control while maintaining airway/breathing reassessment; (3) recognise temperature, calcium, acid–base, platelet/fibrinogen and perfusion trends as team concerns; (4) safely support authorised emergency blood product handling; (5) distinguish trauma-induced coagulopathy (TIC) from a diagnosis based on one INR or D-dimer; and (6) deliver structured serial handover.

## Patient-first nursing response
- **Red flags:** overt/occult bleeding, rapidly evolving poor perfusion, altered mental state, respiratory compromise, new neurological deficit, falling temperature, or ongoing transfusion requirement; vital-sign and laboratory trajectories may matter more than one isolated number.
- **First response:** remain with patient, summon trauma/critical-care help via institutional major-haemorrhage pathway, reassess airway/breathing/circulation and bleeding sites, and support clinician-directed external bleeding control and definitive haemostasis; delegate specimen, documentation and blood-bank tasks without delaying emergency care.
- **Parallel team tasks:** arrange or maintain ordered warming, monitoring, access, oxygenation and laboratory sampling; track temperature, ionised calcium, acid–base/lactate where ordered, platelets, fibrinogen and coagulopathy tests. **No self-prescribed calcium, TXA, blood product ratio, vasopressor, fluid quantity or intervention**.
- **Blood product safety:** confirm patient identity and emergency release rules per the local major-haemorrhage/transfusion policy; report possible reaction, wrong component or mismatch immediately. Do not bypass safety checks merely because haemorrhage is severe.
- **TBI-specific exception:** the permissive-hypotension concepts sometimes applied to haemorrhagic trauma **must not be translated into a blanket blood pressure target for suspected significant brain injury**. Recognise neurological changes, report them promptly, and defer resuscitation targets to the trauma/neurocritical-care team.
- **After haemostasis:** continue reassessment for hidden rebleeding, hypothermia, organ dysfunction, thrombosis risk and transfusion complications. VTE prophylaxis decisions are team-directed and depend on bleeding/neurotrauma status.
- **Evidence nuance:** 2025 German in-hospital trauma guideline evaluates viscoelastic assays and targeted correction; interpretation, component choice and timing belong to authorised clinicians. The literature does not justify giving TXA indiscriminately without indication/context.

## Fictional teaching case (not a scored or published simulator)
**Phase 1:** An injured adult arrives cold, confused and tachycardic; external bleeding appears limited but perfusion is worsening. **Best priority:** assess ABC/perfusion, activate trauma/major bleeding response, track serial observations, initiate authorised monitoring and support source-control evaluation. **Unsafe distractor:** wait for the next haemoglobin or normal blood pressure reading before calling.
**Phase 2:** Blood products are requested while a new neurological deficit is noted. **Best priority:** alert trauma and neurocritical teams; follow emergency transfusion identification rules, temperature/metabolic monitoring and the appropriate TBI-sensitive resuscitation plan. **Unsafe distractor:** apply a generic 'low blood pressure is acceptable' target regardless of suspected brain injury.
**Phase 3:** Repeated tests suggest coagulopathy, and transfusion has been initiated. **Best priority:** communicate the patient’s bleeding/perfusion and serial laboratory trends, treatment already authorised, product identity and any reaction signals. **Unsafe distractor:** diagnose TIC/DIC from a single result and independently choose fibrinogen, plasma, TXA or calcium.
