# Suspected acute variceal haemorrhage
> **UNPUBLISHED PROPOSAL — AI-authored, NOT clinically reviewed.** For educator and hepatology review, not a bedside treatment pathway or independently actionable medication order. This file is deliberately excluded from the original 40-topic and 20-case working edition.

## Why this needs its own ICU nursing lesson
Haematemesis in a patient with cirrhosis or known portal hypertension raises concern for **variceal** bleeding. Ulcer bleeding and portal-hypertensive bleeding share shock/airway emergencies, but variceal bleeding requires a separate, team-led pathway addressing early vasoactive treatment, infection prevention, endoscopic haemostasis and sometimes shunt therapy. **Neither the appearance of blood nor the presence of cirrhosis proves the source**; clinicians confirm the diagnosis.

## Learning objectives
1. Recognise possible variceal haemorrhage and **treat instability as an emergency** even when the source remains uncertain.
2. Explain how ongoing haematemesis, altered consciousness and aspiration risk influence early airway-team escalation and planning for upper endoscopy, without treating intubation as an independent nursing decision.
3. Know the nursing checks for **ordered** vasoactive therapy, antibiotic prophylaxis and blood products; distinguish these from medications used for ulcer bleeding or general ICU stress-ulcer prophylaxis.
4. Recognise and promptly report recurrent bleeding, hypoxaemia, encephalopathy, renal deterioration and perfusion changes.
5. Participate in endoscopy/interventional radiology handovers without independently scoring TIPS eligibility, manipulating balloon tamponade devices, or initiating drugs/doses.

## Patient-first bedside nursing response
**A. Suspected active bleeding:** Remain with the unwell patient, reassess airway, breathing and perfusion, summon GI/hepatology, anaesthesia/critical-care and emergency/major-haemorrhage support per local policy. Communicate blood loss and trend, haemodynamic trajectory, mental state and current therapy. A previously normal haemoglobin **does not exclude significant current blood loss**.

**B. Aspiration/airway:** Repeated fresh haematemesis, impaired alertness, hypoxaemia, poor airway protection or large-volume gastric blood warrant **urgent airway-qualified clinician review**, not a routine pre-endoscopy checklist. Assist with monitoring and authorised aspiration precautions; no blanket statement that every patient requires intubation. If already intubated, preserve ventilator safety and hand over airway concerns.

**C. Therapy coordination, without prescribing:** Baveno VIII recommendation excerpts and prior society guidance continue to identify prompt specialist-directed **vasoactive therapy** and **antimicrobial prophylaxis** in suspected variceal bleeding with cirrhosis. Nurses confirm drug, identity, IV access, timing, contraindication/side-effect concerns, line compatibility and monitoring against the local order and pharmacist review. Do not select between terlipressin, octreotide or alternatives, choose antibiotic, specify dose, or assume empiric PPI alone treats variceal bleeding. Antimicrobial choice also depends on local resistance policy and patient factors.

**D. Resuscitation and coagulopathy:** Track physiological response; coordinate blood bank notification, specimen collection and identity-verified administration under the institution's emergency transfusion pathway. Portal hypertension and liver disease make indiscriminate blood product/volume choices hazardous: one INR or platelet count **is not permission** for nurse-directed plasma or an algorithmic component choice. No target haemoglobin, blood ratios, reversal doses or invasive resuscitation thresholds are supplied.

**E. Endoscopy and rescue:** Following stabilisation/resuscitation, the **Baveno VIII** indexed recommendation addresses upper endoscopy in the early emergency pathway; in ongoing instability, safe resuscitation and a clinician-directed urgent endoscopy plan are concurrent priorities, not competing tasks. Timing is an endoscopist/anaesthesia decision. Higher-risk or refractory cases may require interventional radiology/TIPS assessment; nurses prepare transfer, monitor for deterioration and hand over, **not select TIPS candidates**.

**F. After initial control:** Observe for further haematemesis/melena, altered perfusion, recurrent shock or encephalopathy, renal injury, infection, aspiration, access/transfusion complications. Confirm clinician-defined secondary prophylaxis, endoscopy follow-up and medication plans; do not auto-restart anticoagulants, adjust vasoactive therapies, or discontinue antimicrobials on nursing initiative. Clarify need for tertiary transfer if specialist intervention is unavailable.

## Three-phase fictional case — NOT validated or published
**Phase 1 — recognition.** An adult with cirrhosis vomits fresh blood and becomes drowsy and cool, with a deteriorating heart-rate and blood-pressure trend. **Best response:** stay, reassess ABC/perfusion, urgently summon the ICU/airway/hepatology/major-haemorrhage team and share trend/source uncertainty; delegate authorised blood bank/lab preparation. **Unsafe response:** start routine acid suppression only and wait for the next haemoglobin before contacting a clinician.
**Phase 2 — prescribed treatment.** The team orders a vasoactive medicine and antimicrobial and plans endoscopy. **Best response:** verify orders/allergy/line/site/monitoring, maintain aspiration vigilance, coordinate transfer and safe blood products under protocol. **Unsafe response:** choose vasoactive dosage, give unverified blood products, or declare anticoagulant reversal from an INR alone.
**Phase 3 — recurrent bleed.** Blood reappears after an initial procedure and perfusion deteriorates. **Best response:** re-escalate the emergency response, reassess airway and perfusion, document serial losses/treatments, prepare specialist-directed endoscopy/IR transfer. **Unsafe response:** watch and wait because an endoscopic intervention was already performed.
