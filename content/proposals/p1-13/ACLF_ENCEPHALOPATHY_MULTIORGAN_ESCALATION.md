# ACLF encephalopathy and multiorgan deterioration — nursing
> **UNPUBLISHED PROPOSAL. AI-generated and not independently clinically reviewed.** Not a diagnostic score, treatment pathway, prescribing authority or competency certificate. Not part of the original 40-topic working edition.

## Scope and version alert
This focuses on deterioration in **cirrhosis / advanced chronic liver disease with suspected acute-on-chronic liver failure (ACLF)**. It is **not** a substitute for the separate acute liver failure (ALF) pathway in a patient without known chronic liver disease. An important new version is the **30 October 2026 global multisociety ACLF consensus** (Hepatology and Hepatology International parallel publications). The companion **2026 APASL Beijing position paper** addresses critical care, and the **2026 AASLD–AST adult transplant evaluation guideline** addresses referral. Regional classification approaches differ; do not transpose cutoffs or use one severity label as a nursing diagnosis.

## Objectives
1. Recognise rapid changes in consciousness and safely distinguish **acute encephalopathy** as an observation from **hepatic encephalopathy (HE)** as a clinician-directed diagnosis.
2. Monitor and communicate the simultaneous course of breathing/aspiration protection, perfusion, infection risk, urine output/renal function, blood glucose/electrolytes, coagulation/bleeding and nutrition.
3. Identify common alternative or coexisting causes of decreased alertness: sepsis, stroke/intracranial bleeding, hypoglycaemia, electrolytes, uraemia and sedative/toxic exposure.
4. Use an ordered **serial patient-based assessment** instead of waiting for a calculated severity score or relying on ammonia, INR or bilirubin alone.
5. Escalate impaired airway protection and coordinate transplant-centre discussion promptly where the ICU/hepatology team considers ACLF appropriate.

## Assessment: what an ICU nurse can observe and escalate
| Domain | Nursing observations | Urgent escalation prompts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Neurological / airway | Alertness, ability to respond and protect airway; seizure, new asymmetry; sedation exposures; vomiting or secretions | Decreasing consciousness, focal change, seizure, aspiration/hypoxaemia or lost protective reflexes |
| Circulation | Perfusion, pressure/pulse trends, skin changes, current vasopressor access and delivered therapy | New shock signs, escalating support, suspected infusion failure or access injury |
| Renal / metabolic | Urine output trends, glucose, ordered electrolytes, acid–base and renal results; intake/output and fluid balance | Hypoglycaemia, oliguria, new electrolyte concern or worsening acidosis |
| Infection | Temperature, respiratory/line/abdominal concerns, cultures and antimicrobial administration per orders | Suspected sepsis, worsening haemodynamics or changing mentation |
| Bleeding | New GI bleeding or line/wound oozing; platelet and coagulation trends with physical findings | Bleeding plus instability, new haemorrhagic signs or possible transfusion complication |

**First response to deterioration:** Stay with the patient; reassess airway, breathing and circulation; call urgent ICU/hepatology/airway help if indicated; delegate bedside glucose and prescribed laboratory/monitoring tasks *in parallel*. Do not wait for an ammonia result, a prognostic score, or morning rounds before escalating dangerous changes.

**Terminology safety:** The 2026 ACLF consensus recommends describing altered cognition as **acute encephalopathy**, then investigating whether HE is the primary cause or coexists with infection, stroke, metabolic disturbances or medicines. A raised ammonia may support a differential but does not by itself confirm all altered mental states; a normal result should prompt the treating team to reconsider HE. **Do not conflate cirrhosis-related HE with cerebral oedema and neurological complications of ALF.** No ammonia-triggered treatment or independent airway-intubation threshold is taught.

**Monitoring and handover:** Record serial structured neurological findings (including when sedation makes a test unassessable), ordered glucose/electrolyte trends, renal/urine output, respiratory support, haemodynamic therapy, infection/bleeding findings, response to prescribed care and pending tests. Communicate precipitants such as GI bleeding, infection, bowel disturbance, medicine exposure and dehydration for multidisciplinary evaluation. The team selects diagnostic tests and interventions.

**Medicines and nutrition:** Administer only *authorised* HE, antimicrobial and other treatments per local policy; assess ordered drug/enteral delivery, stool and hydration/electrolyte effects, aspiration risk and feeding interruptions. No lactulose target, drug dose, bowel-regimen adjustment, renal therapy prescription or sedative change is provided here.

**Transplant discussion:** The AASLD–AST 2026 guidance states that patients with ACLF **should be considered for** transplant-centre referral. This is not the same categorical wording as the guideline's **urgent evaluation for all ALF patients**. Nurses escalate possible eligibility discussions to clinicians, document transfer readiness, and do not independently apply listing criteria or prognostic scores.
