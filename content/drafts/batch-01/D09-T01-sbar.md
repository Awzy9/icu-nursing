# SBAR, handover and escalation
> DRAFT – NOT clinically reviewed.

## 1. Overview
SBAR is a structured way to share a patient's condition: Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation/Request. It is flexible – pick the parts needed – and helps a nurse speak up concisely.

## 2. Learning objectives
1. State what each SBAR part contains.
2. Prepare data before calling.
3. Use repeat-back to confirm instructions.
4. Escalate when concern persists.

## 7. Nursing responsibilities
Introduce yourself and the patient; state the problem; give relevant history and numbers; say what you think is happening; ask clearly for what is needed and by when; repeat back the reply.

## 11. Nursing interventions within scope
| Action | Label |
|---|---|
| Structured call, repeat-back, document | Independent nursing |
| Use chain of command per policy if concern remains | Independent nursing (per policy) |
| Orders and treatment decisions | Prescriber decision |

## 12. Escalation example (fictional)
S: "I'm the ICU nurse for bed 4, a 68-year-old man; his blood pressure has dropped and he is newly confused." B: "Day 2 after surgery, on antibiotics, urine output low over 3 hours." A: "I think he is getting worse and may be septic." R: "Please come to see him within 10 minutes. I will repeat observations every 15 minutes." Repeat-back of the reply follows.

## 14. Documentation
Time, person called, content, response, actions, further escalation.

## 17. MCQs
**Q1.** In SBAR, "A" is:
A. Action list. B. The sender's assessment of the problem. C. Admission date. D. Allergy.
**Answer: B.**
**Q2.** Which belongs in Situation?
A. Full history. B. Who you are, patient identifiers, current problem. C. Hospital rules. D. Billing.
**Answer: B.**
**Q3.** After receiving instructions you should:
A. Hang up. B. Repeat them back to confirm. C. Ignore. D. Delay.
**Answer: B.**
**Q4.** SBAR must always use all four parts in order.
A. True. B. False – adaptable, like a menu. C. True for nurses only. D. False; never use it.
**Answer: B.**
**Q5.** Your concern persists after review. Next step?
A. Stop. B. Escalate per chain of command. C. Wait. D. Hide it.
**Answer: B.**
