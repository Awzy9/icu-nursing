# Preventing CLABSI
> DRAFT – NOT clinically reviewed. The 2011 guideline is dated; a reviewer must confirm current practice.

## 1. Overview
A central line is a route from skin to bloodstream. Prevention is mainly consistent daily care by nurses: hand hygiene, aseptic access, site care, and questioning whether the line is still needed.

## 2. Learning objectives
1. Describe the nursing role in line maintenance.
2. Identify when to perform hand hygiene around the catheter.
3. Know that dressing integrity and daily site checks matter.
4. Recognise signs that need escalation.
5. Separate nursing actions from prescriber decisions (insertion, removal, antibiotics).

## 7. Nursing responsibilities (from the 2011 guideline, rewritten)
- Only trained, competent staff handle lines (Category IA).
- Hand hygiene before and after palpating the site and before/after accessing, replacing, repairing or dressing the catheter (IB).
- Aseptic technique for all care (IB).
- Check the insertion site daily by touch through the dressing or by inspection (II); do not remove opaque/gauze dressings if no signs of infection.
- Replace a dressing that is damp, loosened or soiled; gauze every 2 days, transparent at least every 7 days for short-term CVC, per the guideline (rated IB/II); follow local policy.
- Skin antisepsis at dressing changes with >0.5% chlorhexidine in alcohol unless contraindicated (IA).
- Ask daily whether each line is still essential; promptly removing lines no longer needed is Category IA – the **removal order is a prescriber decision**, the nurse raises the question.

## 10. Warning signs and red flags
Fever or rigors; new tenderness, redness or discharge at the site; line dysfunction; unexplained instability; wet/loose dressing.

## 11. Nursing interventions within scope
| Action | Label |
|---|---|
| Hand hygiene, dressing integrity, site inspection | Independent nursing |
| Dressing change using sterile technique per policy | Independent nursing (competency required) |
| Take blood cultures, start antibiotics, remove/replace line | Needs order/protocol or prescriber decision |

## 12. Escalation
S: Patient with CVC has fever/site changes. B: Line day X, site findings, vitals. A: Possible line infection or sepsis. R: Please review now; consider cultures and line plan.

## 14. Documentation
Daily site check; dressing change date/time and reason; line necessity discussion; escalation.

## 17. MCQs
**Q1.** Best time for hand hygiene around a central line?
A. Only after contact. B. Before and after palpating or accessing the site. C. Only wearing gloves. D. Once per shift.
**Answer: B.**

**Q2.** The dressing over a CVC is damp and lifting. Next action?
A. Tape over it. B. Replace it using aseptic technique per policy. C. Wait until the next shift. D. Remove the line.
**Answer: B.** D is a prescriber decision.

**Q3.** Who decides to remove a line that is no longer needed?
A. The nurse alone. B. The prescriber, after the nurse raises the question. C. The patient. D. Nobody.
**Answer: B.**

**Q4.** A patient with a CVC develops rigors and redness at the site. First step?
A. Document only. B. Assess, escalate with SBAR, and prepare for orders. C. Give antibiotics without orders. D. Remove line yourself.
**Answer: B.**

**Q5.** Daily site assessment may be done by:
A. Looking only. B. Touching through the dressing or inspecting a transparent dressing. C. Peeling every dressing daily. D. Not needed.
**Answer: B.**
