# Recognising possible sepsis and escalating
> DRAFT – NOT clinically reviewed. UK-derived thresholds; reviewers must check international applicability.

## 1. Overview
Sepsis can present vaguely, often without fever. The nurse's role is to suspect it, assess with a structured set of observations, grade the risk with a validated score where used, and escalate quickly. Treatment decisions belong to the prescriber/team.

## 2. Learning objectives
1. Describe non-specific presentations and why family concerns matter.
2. List risk factors that raise vulnerability.
3. Explain that NEWS2 bands help rank urgency in adults in acute settings (NICE: 7 or more high, 5–6 medium, 1–4 low, 0 very low; a single parameter scoring 3 prompts urgent clinician review).
4. State that skin signs and trajectory can mean higher risk than the score alone.
5. Name the re-assessment frequency suggested by risk level.

## 7. Nursing responsibilities
- Ask "could this be sepsis?" for any possible infection; take family/carer concerns seriously, especially changes from usual behaviour.
- Take extra care where history is hard (interpreter needs, cognitive impairment).
- Record structured observations and calculate/read NEWS2.
- Repeat observations: NICE cites every 30 min (high risk), hourly (medium), 4–6 hourly (low), per local protocol (very low).

## 10. Warning signs and red flags
New confusion; fast breathing; low blood pressure; fast heart rate; low urine output; mottled/ashen skin, cyanosis, non-blanching rash; deterioration despite treatment.

## 11. Nursing interventions within scope
| Action | Label |
|---|---|
| Full set of observations, NEWS2, escalate by policy | Independent nursing |
| Collect cultures, lactate, give fluids/antimicrobials | Needs order/protocol |
| Decide diagnosis, antimicrobial choice, fluid volume | Prescriber decision |

## 12. Escalation
S: Patient with suspected infection, NEWS2 [x] (or single parameter 3). B: Source, risk factors, trend. A: I am concerned about sepsis. R: Please review within [time per policy]; I will repeat observations every [interval].

## 14. Documentation
Observations and score with times; who was called and when; response; reassessment times.

## 17. MCQs
**Q1.** Which presentation should make a nurse think of sepsis?
A. Only fever. B. Non-specific unwell state with possible infection. C. Normal observations always. D. Only positive cultures.
**Answer: B.**

**Q2.** NEWS2 is 7 in a patient with suspected infection. This suggests:
A. Low risk. B. High risk; urgent senior escalation. C. Very low risk. D. Discharge.
**Answer: B.**

**Q3.** One parameter scores 3 but total is 4. Best action?
A. Ignore. B. Request an urgent clinician review per policy. C. Wait 12 hours. D. Treat alone.
**Answer: B.**

**Q4.** Mottled skin with NEWS2 of 4. The nurse should:
A. Trust the score only. B. Consider risk higher than the score and escalate. C. Reduce observation. D. Discharge.
**Answer: B.**

**Q5.** Who chooses the antimicrobial?
A. Nurse. B. Prescriber/team per protocol. C. Patient. D. Porter.
**Answer: B.**
