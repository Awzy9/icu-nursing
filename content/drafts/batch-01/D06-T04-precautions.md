# Standard and transmission-based precautions in the ICU
> DRAFT – NOT clinically reviewed. Not for learners until qualified reviewers approve.

## 1. Overview
Standard precautions are the baseline infection-prevention practices for **every** patient, whatever their diagnosis. Transmission-based precautions are added when a pathogen's route of spread needs more (contact, droplet, airborne). Local policy and the infection-prevention team define exact requirements.

## 2. Learning objectives
1. List the elements of standard precautions.
2. Choose, in principle, the added measures for contact, droplet and airborne routes.
3. Explain that PPE choice follows a risk assessment of the expected exposure.
4. Recognise what the nurse can do alone and what needs infection-prevention input.

## 3. Why this matters in ICU nursing
ICU patients have invasive devices and impaired defences, and nurses have the most frequent contact. Consistent precautions protect patients, staff and visitors.

## 7. Nursing responsibilities
- Hand hygiene at the moments defined by your policy.
- Select PPE by anticipated contact with blood, body fluids, secretions or contaminated surfaces.
- Respiratory hygiene; safe injection and sharps practice; equipment and environment cleaning; safe linen handling.
- Apply the isolation sign/precaution level in force and keep it visible.

## 10. Warning signs and red flags
- New diarrhoea, productive cough, rash, or fever in a patient not on precautions.
- Supplies missing at the room entrance; PPE breaches; exposure incident.

## 11. Nursing interventions within scope
| Action | Scope label |
|---|---|
| Apply standard precautions to all patients | Independent nursing |
| Start precautions when a recognised trigger appears, per policy | Independent nursing (per local policy) |
| Choose room type, airborne isolation room, discontinuing isolation | Needs infection-prevention/protocol decision |
| Antimicrobial treatment | Prescriber decision |

Added measures by route (summary of the CDC page): **contact** – gown and gloves, single room if available; **droplet** – mask on entering, patient masked for transport; **airborne** – airborne-infection isolation room, fit-tested N95 or higher, limit transport.

## 12. Escalation criteria
S: Patient in bed X has [symptom] and no precautions in place. B: [exposure/history, devices]. A: Possible [route] pathogen. R: Please advise precaution level and testing; I have started [interim measures per policy].

## 14. Documentation
Precaution type and start time; who was informed; PPE breaches/exposures reported; patient/visitor education.

## 17. MCQs
**Q1 (application).** A patient with new watery diarrhoea is not on isolation. Which is the best first nursing action under your policy?
A. Wait for the physician round. B. Apply the contact-precaution measures in policy and notify infection prevention. C. Move the patient to the corridor. D. Use only hand gel and no gown.
**Answer: B.** The nurse starts the interim policy measures and escalates. A delays; C is unsafe; D under-protects against contact spread (policy normally requires gown/gloves, and local rules on hand hygiene product apply).

**Q2 (understanding).** Standard precautions apply to:
A. Only patients with known infections. B. Only patients in isolation. C. All patients. D. Only during procedures.
**Answer: C.** They are the baseline for all care.

**Q3 (safety).** Which PPE is chosen for a task?
A. The same set for every task. B. According to the expected exposure. C. Whatever is closest. D. Only gloves in all cases.
**Answer: B.**

**Q4 (judgment).** Airborne precautions are ordered. Which is correct in principle?
A. Surgical mask and open door. B. Airborne-infection isolation room and fit-tested respirator. C. No mask needed. D. Visit routine transport freely.
**Answer: B.**

**Q5 (scenario).** You are unsure of the precaution level for a new admission. What should you do?
A. Guess. B. Ask the infection-prevention team or follow the admission screening policy. C. Ignore. D. Ask the patient's family to decide.
**Answer: B.**
