# Adult Advanced Cardiovascular Life Support (ACLS) for ICU nurses

> DRAFT – NOT clinically reviewed. For learning and simulation.

## 1. Overview
During an adult code, effective team coordination keeps CPR and rhythm-directed treatment moving. This lesson covers bedside tasks during arrest, unstable rhythms with a pulse, and initial care after return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC).

## 2. Learning objectives
Distinguish shockable and nonshockable arrest, identify instability in rhythms with a pulse, and coordinate the first minutes after ROSC.

## 6. Clinical assessment
Ventricular fibrillation and pulseless ventricular tachycardia are shockable. Asystole and pulseless electrical activity (PEA) are nonshockable. An organised tracing without a pulse is PEA. With a pulse, assess perfusion and report hypotension, altered mental status, signs of shock, ischemic chest discomfort or acute heart failure.

## 7. Nursing responsibilities
Keep CPR going between rhythm assessments. Under local ACLS policy, support prompt defibrillation for shockable arrest. For nonshockable arrest, help prepare early epinephrine and gather clues to reversible causes such as hypoxia, bleeding, electrolyte disturbance, tension pneumothorax, tamponade or thrombosis. For shockable arrest, the AHA places epinephrine after initial defibrillation attempts have failed. Repeat back medication orders and announce when administration is complete.

## 10. Warning signs
Recheck the patient when a monitor rhythm changes. A pacing spike or an organised ECG tracing alone cannot establish perfusion. Report a sudden rise in end-tidal CO₂ during CPR as a possible sign of ROSC and confirm circulation with the team at an appropriate assessment.

## 11. Nursing interventions within scope
**Independent nursing actions within local policy** include continuing CPR, preparing equipment and reporting changes. For unstable monomorphic wide-complex tachycardia with a pulse, prepare for synchronized cardioversion when instability is attributable to the rhythm. Sustained polymorphic VT calls for an unsynchronized shock. For poorly perfusing bradycardia, prepare for prescribed therapy and possible transcutaneous pacing, and help verify mechanical capture if pacing is used. Rhythm diagnosis, shock settings, medication selection and dose follow local protocols and the team leader or prescriber.

## 12. Escalation
After ROSC, immediately report recurrent arrhythmia, hypotension or worsening oxygenation. Support airway confirmation, reliable oxygen and ventilation assessment, blood pressure monitoring and a 12-lead ECG. The team sets oxygen, ventilation, perfusion and temperature goals.

## 14. Documentation
Track arrests and ROSC times, rhythms, shocks and energy, medications and times, airway confirmation, suspected causes and the post-arrest handoff.

## 15. Practical bedside example
A patient has an organised rhythm but no pulse. The team continues CPR and prepares epinephrine. The bedside nurse reports recent blood loss and checks access while the leader evaluates hypovolemia. When perfusion returns, the nurse records ROSC time and helps obtain continuous blood pressure monitoring and a 12-lead ECG.

## 17. MCQs
**Q1 (Application).** An unresponsive ICU patient has an organised rhythm and no pulse. Which finding should you communicate?

A. Stable rhythm with residual low flow
B. Ventricular fibrillation requiring immediate shock
C. Pulseless electrical activity requiring continued CPR
D. Perfusing tachycardia requiring synchronized cardioversion

**Answer: C.**

**Why correct:** PEA requires CPR and treatment directed at nonshockable arrest.
**Why not A:** An organised rhythm cannot establish perfusion.
**Why not B:** Ventricular fibrillation lacks an organised rhythm.
**Why not D:** Cardioversion for perfusing tachycardia presupposes a pulse.

**Q2 (Application).** The team identifies ventricular fibrillation. Which action should the nurse help expedite?

A. Placement of an advanced airway before shock
B. A twelve lead ECG before rhythm treatment
C. Waiting for vascular access before shocking
D. Prompt defibrillation with minimal CPR interruption

**Answer: D.**

**Why correct:** VF is shockable and prolonged pauses diminish CPR quality.
**Why not A:** Airway placement should not delay defibrillation.
**Why not B:** A diagnostic ECG should not delay a shock.
**Why not C:** Vascular access can be pursued without delaying defibrillation.

**Q3 (Application).** The code leader identifies asystole while compressions are underway. What preparation fits this rhythm?

A. Prepare early epinephrine under protocol
B. Prepare synchronized shock delivery for asystole
C. Hold medications until after the first shock
D. Prepare elective cardioversion after airway placement

**Answer: A.**

**Why correct:** The AHA supports epinephrine as soon as feasible in nonshockable arrest.
**Why not B:** Synchronized cardioversion is not an asystole treatment.
**Why not C:** Asystole is a nonshockable rhythm.
**Why not D:** Elective cardioversion does not treat asystole.

**Q4 (Application).** A patient with a pulse has monomorphic wide-complex tachycardia causing hypotension and confusion. What should you prepare for?

A. A diagnostic ECG before activating urgent help
B. Prompt synchronized cardioversion
C. A pacing trial before treating the tachycardia
D. A fluid challenge as the sole immediate treatment

**Answer: B.**

**Why correct:** Synchronized cardioversion treats unstable monomorphic wide-complex tachycardia with a pulse.
**Why not A:** Unstable tachycardia needs immediate treatment and escalation.
**Why not C:** Pacing is used for selected bradyarrhythmias.
**Why not D:** Supportive fluids alone do not address the unstable rhythm.

**Q5 (Application).** During transcutaneous pacing, spikes and paced complexes appear. What finding supports effective mechanical capture?

A. Pacing spikes visible on the rhythm strip
B. A higher pacing output shown on the device
C. Arterial pulsations matching the paced rhythm
D. Paced complexes with a wider QRS morphology

**Answer: C.**

**Why correct:** Arterial pulsations indicate that paced beats are generating flow.
**Why not A:** Spikes show pacing output, not mechanical perfusion.
**Why not B:** Device output alone cannot show mechanical capture.
**Why not D:** QRS morphology may demonstrate electrical capture only.

**Q6 (Application).** After ROSC, which set of tasks best supports immediate stabilisation?

A. Delay blood pressure assessment until transfer
B. Wean oxygen before monitoring becomes reliable
C. Prioritise neurological prognosis before obtaining vital signs
D. Assess airway, oxygenation and circulation

**Answer: D.**

**Why correct:** Immediate reassessment helps identify airway, respiratory and haemodynamic problems.
**Why not A:** Hypotension can recur and requires prompt recognition.
**Why not B:** Oxygen titration needs reliable oxygenation measurement.
**Why not C:** Early stabilisation precedes formal neurological prognostication.
