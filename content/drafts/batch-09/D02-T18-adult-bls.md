# Adult Basic Life Support (BLS) for ICU nurses

> DRAFT – NOT clinically reviewed. For learning and simulation.

## 1. Overview
The first bedside responder can shorten the time to chest compressions and defibrillation. Practice recognising arrest, calling the hospital code team, and maintaining CPR quality while equipment arrives.

## 2. Learning objectives
Recognise adult cardiac and respiratory arrest, deliver high-quality CPR, and coordinate AED use and handoff.

## 6. Clinical assessment
For an unresponsive adult, activate the hospital response and have someone bring an AED or defibrillator. Check breathing and pulse together for up to 10 seconds. Gasping is abnormal. If breathing is absent or abnormal and you cannot definitely feel a pulse, start CPR. With a definite pulse and absent or inadequate breathing, give one breath every 6 seconds and recheck the pulse every 2 minutes.

## 7. Nursing responsibilities
Compress in the centre of the chest over the lower half of the sternum at 100–120/min. Aim for at least 5 cm in an average adult and avoid exceeding 6 cm. Allow the chest to recoil fully. Before an advanced airway, give 30 compressions followed by 2 breaths. Give each breath over about 1 second, enough for visible chest rise. Keep pauses brief and change compressors about every 2 minutes, or sooner if fatigued.

## 10. Warning signs
Agonal gasps can look like breathing. Watch for leaning on the chest, shallow compressions, excessive ventilation and pauses while staff exchange roles.

## 11. Nursing interventions within scope
**Independent nursing actions within local policy** include activating the code team, starting CPR and coordinating a compressor change. Keep compressions going during AED pad placement when feasible. Clear contact during analysis and shock delivery. Resume compressions promptly after a shock or a no-shock prompt in ongoing arrest. Advanced airway and prescriber decisions follow the designated team roles.

## 12. Escalation
Give the team the collapse time, whether a pulse was felt, when compressions started, and whether an AED shock was delivered.

## 14. Documentation
Record when the patient was found, code activation, CPR start, rhythm checks, shocks and transfer of care.

## 15. Practical bedside example
A monitored ICU patient becomes unresponsive and gasps. You cannot clearly feel a pulse during the brief assessment. A colleague activates the code and brings the AED while you start compressions. The team prepares a replacement compressor during the first cycle.

## 17. MCQs
**Q1 (Application).** An unresponsive patient gasps and has no definite pulse after a brief check. What now?

A. Start chest compressions while help arrives
B. Deliver rescue breaths and recheck after two minutes
C. Obtain a twelve lead ECG before intervening
D. Continue checking for a pulse until certain

**Answer: A.**

**Why correct:** Gasping and no definite pulse warrant CPR.
**Why not B:** Rescue breathing alone is for a definite pulse.
**Why not C:** An ECG would delay initial chest compressions.
**Why not D:** Prolonged pulse checks delay initiation of CPR.

**Q2 (Application).** An apnoeic adult has a definite pulse. What support is indicated initially?

A. Begin thirty compressions followed by two breaths
B. Give one breath every six seconds
C. Give one breath every two seconds
D. Wait for the airway team before ventilating

**Answer: B.**

**Why correct:** Support ventilation and reassess the pulse every two minutes.
**Why not A:** Compressions are indicated if the pulse is lost.
**Why not C:** Rapid ventilation exceeds the recommended frequency.
**Why not D:** Apnoea requires ventilation while help is coming.

**Q3 (Application).** The feedback device shows 132 compressions per minute. What correction is appropriate?

A. Continue at this rate if depth is adequate
B. Increase the rate during AED preparation
C. Bring the rate to 100–120 per minute
D. Reduce the rate to 70–90 per minute

**Answer: C.**

**Why correct:** This brings the rate into the adult target range.
**Why not A:** Adequate depth does not correct the excessive rate.
**Why not B:** Pad preparation does not change the rate target.
**Why not D:** This reduction would put the rate below target.

**Q4 (Application).** The AED has delivered a shock. The patient remains motionless. What comes next?

A. Pause for an immediate twelve lead ECG
B. Remove the pads to inspect skin contact
C. Perform a prolonged pulse check before resuming
D. Resume chest compressions promptly

**Answer: D.**

**Why correct:** Prompt CPR limits the interruption after a shock.
**Why not A:** An ECG delays CPR while arrest may be ongoing.
**Why not B:** Pads should remain available for the next analysis.
**Why not C:** Routine immediate pulse checks add unnecessary pauses.

**Q5 (Application).** A compressor leans on the chest between pushes. What coaching is appropriate?

A. Allow full recoil between chest compressions
B. Maintain pressure to hold the hands steady
C. Increase depth while maintaining baseline pressure
D. Slow the rate without releasing resting pressure

**Answer: A.**

**Why correct:** Full recoil helps venous return between compressions.
**Why not B:** Continuous pressure impairs full chest recoil.
**Why not C:** Extra depth does not fix incomplete recoil.
**Why not D:** A slower rate does not release pressure on the chest.

**Q6 (Application).** With no advanced airway in place, what compression-to-ventilation pattern is appropriate for trained staff?

A. Give two breaths after every fifteen compressions
B. Give two breaths after every thirty compressions
C. Give five breaths after every thirty compressions
D. Deliver continuous breaths during all compressions

**Answer: B.**

**Why correct:** This is the adult BLS ratio before an advanced airway.
**Why not A:** This is not the standard adult BLS ratio.
**Why not C:** Five breaths add unnecessary interruptions and ventilation.
**Why not D:** This is not the standard approach before an advanced airway.
