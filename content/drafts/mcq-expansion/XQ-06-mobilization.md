# Safe mobilization of ventilated patients (Hodgson 2014)

## 17. MCQs
**Q1 (judgment).** The physiotherapist asks whether an intubated patient can sit on the edge of the bed. A colleague says the endotracheal tube rules it out. What does the consensus say?
A. The colleague is right: a patient with an endotracheal tube should stay in bed until extubated.
B. Mobilization is allowed after tracheostomy, because the tube is then more secure.
C. Mobilization is allowed in bed but not out of bed while a tube is in place.
D. An endotracheal tube is not in itself a contraindication to early mobilization.
**Answer: D.**
**Why correct:** An endotracheal tube alone does not exclude active mobilization. The 2025 PADIS focused update conditionally favors enhanced rehabilitation compared with usual care, but a multidisciplinary safety and tolerance assessment remains necessary.
**Why not A:** The consensus says the opposite.
**Why not B:** The consensus does not require a tracheostomy.
**Why not C:** The respiratory criteria apply to in- and out-of-bed mobilization.
**Trace:** N-HODGSON-2014, S-SCCM-PADIS — Intubation alone is not a contraindication; conditional enhanced rehabilitation recommendation.

**Q2 (safety).** Before each mobilization session for a ventilated patient, which checks does the consensus describe?
A. That the patient has had a full meal, so they have the energy for the activity.
B. That the family is present, so they can reassure the patient during the activity.
C. That any artificial airway is correctly positioned and secure, and that oxygen is available with a reserve longer than the expected activity.
D. That the sedation has been stopped for at least a full day beforehand.
**Answer: C.**
**Why correct:** The consensus says an appropriate professional should check before each episode that any artificial airway is correctly positioned and secure and that supplemental oxygen has a reserve exceeding the expected duration.
**Why not A:** This is not a safety check described in the consensus.
**Why not B:** Family presence is not a safety criterion.
**Why not D:** This is not a criterion the consensus describes.
**Trace:** N-HODGSON-2014 — Check airway position/security and oxygen reserve before each episode.

**Q3 (judgment).** A patient's oxygen saturation, FiO2 and PEEP are all at the edge of the unit's safety limits at once, and the team wants to sit him out. What does the consensus advise?
A. Go ahead, because each value is still within its own limit when looked at one by one.
B. Consult an experienced medical team before mobilizing.
C. Go ahead and mobilize more slowly than usual so the patient has time to adapt to the change.
D. Cancel all mobilization for the rest of the admission, since the limits were reached once.
**Answer: B.**
**Why correct:** The consensus says that if the patient is at the safety limits for several categories (for example low saturation, high FiO2 and high PEEP), an experienced medical team should be consulted before mobilization.
**Why not A:** The consensus asks for a senior discussion when several limits are reached together.
**Why not C:** Slowing down is not the advice given.
**Why not D:** The advice is to consult, not to cancel permanently.
**Trace:** N-HODGSON-2014 — At limits in several categories: consult experienced medical team.

**Q4 (scope).** A ventilated patient on a vasoactive infusion has had his dose increased twice this morning. A colleague says any vasoactive drug means no mobilization. What is the most accurate view?
A. Vasoactive drugs are not an absolute contraindication, but rising doses call for caution or contraindicate mobilization, and the unit decides case by case.
B. The colleague is right: any vasoactive drug means mobilization is not allowed.
C. A single threshold dose is agreed worldwide and the nurse can look it up in the paper.
D. Rising doses are irrelevant as long as the blood pressure reading on the monitor looks acceptable.
**Answer: A.**
**Why correct:** The consensus found vasoactive drugs per se were not an absolute contraindication; appropriateness depends on the dose, the change in dose (rising doses call for caution or contraindication) and perfusion, and units agree thresholds case by case.
**Why not B:** The consensus does not say this.
**Why not C:** The panel could not agree a threshold dose.
**Why not D:** The change in dose and perfusion are both part of the consideration.
**Trace:** N-HODGSON-2014 — Vasoactive drugs not absolute contraindication; dose, change in dose and perfusion matter; no consensus on a threshold.

**Q5 (application).** A patient has an oxygen saturation of 94%, FiO2 of 0.45 and a respiratory rate of 22 and no other contraindication. How do these respiratory values compare with the consensus criteria?
A. They do not meet the criteria, because the FiO2 must be at room air before any mobilization.
B. They meet the criteria only for in-bed exercises, never for sitting out of bed.
C. They are irrelevant, as the consensus gives no respiratory values for safe mobilization.
D. They fit the historical respiratory screen, but the team must assess overall readiness before mobilization.
**Answer: D.**
**Why correct:** These observations meet the respiratory examples in the 2014 consensus; they are not stand-alone clearance. Check cardiovascular, neurological and other risks and current stability with the team. Use local criteria, not these historical thresholds as universal rules.
**Why not A:** The criterion is FiO2 below 0.6, not room air.
**Why not B:** The criteria apply to in- and out-of-bed mobilization.
**Why not C:** The consensus gives those values.
**Trace:** N-HODGSON-2014 — FiO2 <0.6, SpO2 >90%, RR <30 considered safe criteria if no other contraindications.

**Q6 (scope).** A nurse asks whether the consensus criteria mean she can mobilize ventilated patients without the team's judgement. What is the best answer?
A. Yes, once the numbers are met she may mobilize alone without any team agreement.
B. Yes, because the criteria were issued by a regulator and replace local policy.
C. No: the criteria are a guide to be used with clinical judgement, and each unit should consider them against its own staffing and expertise.
D. No, because the criteria apply to patients who are not receiving any oxygen.
**Answer: C.**
**Why correct:** The 2014 criteria guide experienced clinicians rather than replace judgment. In the 2022 TEAM trial, a higher level of early active mobilization did not improve days alive outside hospital and produced more potentially related adverse events. The 2025 PADIS conditional recommendation does not mandate a universal activity intensity. Individualize progression and stop for deterioration.
**Why not A:** The consensus does not remove the need for judgement and team input.
**Why not B:** They are expert consensus, not a regulatory standard.
**Why not D:** The criteria were written for ventilated patients.
**Trace:** N-HODGSON-2014, S-TEAM-RCT-2022, S-SCCM-PADIS — Historical screening consensus, TEAM trial results and conditional enhanced rehabilitation guidance.
