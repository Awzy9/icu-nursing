## 17. MCQs
**Q1 (safety).** Before giving intravenous midazolam to a patient for a procedure on the unit, what must be in place according to the label's boxed warning?
A. Continuous respiratory and cardiac monitoring, resuscitation equipment on hand, and skilled airway staff.
B. A signed consent form and a quiet room, because the drug is routine and low risk.
C. A nurse checking the patient's colour every fifteen minutes during the whole recovery period.
D. The patient's family at the bedside to watch for changes in alertness while the nurse is busy.
**Answer: A.**
**Why correct:** The boxed warning says IV midazolam should be used only where continuous monitoring of respiratory and cardiac function is provided, with immediately available resuscitative drugs, equipment for bag-valve-mask ventilation and intubation, and personnel skilled in airway management.
**Why not B:** Consent and a quiet room are not the safety measures the warning names.
**Why not C:** Intermittent checks are not continuous monitoring.
**Why not D:** Family cannot replace trained monitoring.
**Trace:** L-MIDAZOLAM — Boxed warning: respiratory depression; continuous monitoring; resuscitation equipment; skilled personnel.

**Q2 (judgment).** A patient already receiving an opioid infusion is prescribed a benzodiazepine as well. What should the nurse understand about this combination?
A. It is a routine combination with no added risk, so standard observations are enough.
B. It is safe if the two drugs are given at least ten minutes apart in the same patient.
C. It matters only in patients who have never had an opioid before this admission.
D. It can cause profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma and death.
**Answer: D.**
**Why correct:** The opioid boxed warnings say concomitant use with benzodiazepines or other CNS depressants may cause profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma and death.
**Why not A:** The labels warn of a specific added risk.
**Why not B:** The risk concerns concomitant use, not the gap between doses.
**Why not C:** The warning applies broadly.
**Trace:** L-FENTANYL, L-MORPHINE, L-MIDAZOLAM — Boxed warnings: concomitant opioids/benzodiazepines/CNS depressants.

**Q3 (safety).** A nurse is about to draw a second dose from a propofol vial that was opened for a previous patient 'because there is plenty left'. What is the correct response?
A. It is acceptable if the vial has been kept in the fridge and the stopper wiped with alcohol.
B. It is acceptable for the same ward and the same day, because the emulsion stays sterile for a full shift.
C. Do not use it: the label says vials are never accessed more than once or used on more than one person, and unused product is discarded.
D. It is acceptable if a new needle is used, since the needle is the main source of contamination.
**Answer: C.**
**Why correct:** The label states strict aseptic technique must be maintained, vials are never to be accessed more than once or used on more than one person, and unused drug product should be discarded.
**Why not A:** The label does not allow reuse for another patient under any storage.
**Why not B:** The label sets time limits and forbids use on more than one person.
**Why not D:** A new needle does not remove the contamination risk of a reused vial.
**Trace:** L-PROPOFOL — Label: strict aseptic technique; vials never accessed more than once or used on more than one person; discard unused.

**Q4 (monitoring).** A patient sedated with dexmedetomidine has a heart rate that falls and a blood pressure that drops over twenty minutes. What does the label support?
A. Treat it as the intended sedative effect and record the readings at the next routine check.
B. Report promptly, since bradycardia and hypotension may need medical intervention.
C. Increase the drug to deepen sedation so the patient settles and the readings steady.
D. Stop monitoring the heart rhythm to avoid false alarms while the patient is sleeping.
**Answer: B.**
**Why correct:** The label warns of hypotension, bradycardia and sinus arrest, which may need medical intervention, and says patients should be continuously monitored.
**Why not A:** The label asks for reporting, not acceptance of falling vital signs.
**Why not C:** Dose changes are a prescriber or protocol decision, and the readings suggest a problem.
**Why not D:** Continuous monitoring is required.
**Trace:** L-DEXMEDETOMIDINE — Label: continuous monitoring; hypotension, bradycardia and sinus arrest.

**Q5 (application).** An elderly, frail patient with chronic lung disease has just received a morphine dose. Which preparation does the label support?
A. Close respiratory monitoring, and naloxone and resuscitation equipment immediately available.
B. Routine observations once a shift, because morphine is a very familiar drug on the unit.
C. Keep the patient flat and covered so they sleep more soundly after the dose.
D. Give a second sedative with it to prevent any restlessness after the pain has gone.
**Answer: A.**
**Why correct:** The label warns of life-threatening respiratory depression in patients with chronic pulmonary disease or the elderly, cachectic or debilitated and advises close monitoring, and has naloxone and resuscitative equipment immediately available where cardiovascular instability is a risk.
**Why not B:** Familiarity does not lower the risk in this patient.
**Why not C:** Sleeping soundly is a warning sign, not a goal.
**Why not D:** Adding a CNS depressant raises the risk.
**Trace:** L-MORPHINE — Label: close monitoring in chronic pulmonary disease/elderly/debilitated; naloxone and resuscitative equipment available.

**Q6 (judgment).** Shortly after a rapid fentanyl dose, a patient's chest becomes stiff and ventilation is difficult. What is the correct first response?
A. Reassure the patient and wait for the stiffness to wear off before doing anything further.
B. Give a second opioid dose to relieve the discomfort and relax the chest muscles.
C. Remove the pulse oximeter so the alarm stops while the team is called.
D. Call for help immediately; the label warns of muscle rigidity that the prescriber manages.
**Answer: D.**
**Why correct:** The label lists the risk of skeletal muscle rigidity and says it is managed with a neuromuscular blocking agent, which is a prescriber decision.
**Why not A:** Rigidity that limits ventilation is an emergency.
**Why not B:** Another opioid dose does not treat rigidity and may worsen it.
**Why not C:** Monitoring is essential during the event.
**Trace:** L-FENTANYL — Label: skeletal muscle rigidity; managed with neuromuscular blocking agent.
