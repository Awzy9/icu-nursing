# Chest drain monitoring (BTS clinical statement 2023)
> DRAFT – NOT clinically reviewed. AI-generated. Unpublished; never imported into the question bank.

## 17. MCQs
**Q1 (application).** You are moving a patient with an underwater-seal chest drain bottle from bed to chair. Where should the bottle be?
A. Below the insertion site at all times, kept upright.
B. Beside the patient on the chair, level with the drain site, so the tubing is not stretched.
C. On the patient's lap, tilted slightly, so it cannot be knocked over during the transfer.
D. Hung from the drip stand above the chair, which makes the readings easier to see.
**Answer: A.**
**Why correct:** The BTS statement says the drain bottle must be kept below the insertion site at all times and the drain must be kept upright.
**Why not B:** A bottle at the insertion level risks fluid or air returning to the pleural space.
**Why not C:** A tilted bottle can break the water seal.
**Why not D:** Raising the bottle above the insertion site is unsafe.
**Trace:** N-BTS-PCS-2023 — Bottle below insertion site; keep drain upright.

**Q2 (judgment).** During controlled drainage of a large pleural effusion, a patient develops repetitive coughing and new chest pain. The drain is being used for fluid removal, not treatment of a bubbling pneumothorax. What is the most appropriate response?
A. Encourage the patient to keep coughing to clear the lungs and recheck the drainage in an hour.
B. Increase the drainage by lowering the bottle, so the effusion clears faster and the symptoms settle.
C. Give the next analgesia and observe, since coughing is common while a lung re-expands.
D. Stop effusion drainage per plan, assess and urgently alert the pleural team.
**Answer: D.**
**Why correct:** Repetitive cough or chest pain during drainage of a large effusion can warn of a serious complication including re-expansion pulmonary oedema. BTS advises prompt clamping of the effusion drain in this setting. Follow the written fluid-drainage instructions and assess the patient immediately; do not apply this response to a bubbling pneumothorax drain.
**Why not A:** The symptoms may signal re-expansion pulmonary oedema.
**Why not B:** Faster drainage is the opposite of what the symptoms call for.
**Why not C:** Analgesia alone does not address the possibility of a drainage-related complication. Assess breathing and circulation and escalate promptly while following the written effusion-drainage plan.
**Trace:** N-BTS-PCS-2023 — Clamp promptly with repetitive coughing/chest pain (RPO); controlled drainage of large effusions.

**Q3 (safety).** The drain bottle is bubbling and a colleague wants to clamp the tubing before taking the patient for transport. What is the best response?
A. Clamp it close to the chest wall, which is safe for a short journey and keeps the bottle level.
B. Clamp it and remove the bottle from the circuit so the patient can be moved on a clear trolley.
C. Do not clamp it; clamping a bubbling drain should be avoided unless under specialist pleural supervision, so seek instruction.
D. Clamp it while the lift is moving, because the motion is the main risk to the seal.
**Answer: C.**
**Why correct:** Clamping an actively bubbling pneumothorax drain can cause dangerous pressure accumulation. Avoid routine transport clamping; seek specialist guidance for exceptional circumstances and follow the prescribed drainage and transport plan.
**Why not A:** A clamped bubbling drain can allow air to build up in the pleural space.
**Why not B:** Disconnecting an unsupervised, clamped drain adds risk.
**Why not D:** Clamping a bubbling drain is not made safe by the length of time.
**Trace:** N-BTS-PCS-2023 — Clamping a bubbling chest tube should be avoided unless under specialist pleural supervision.

**Q4 (judgment).** A patient with a conventional underwater-seal drain for pneumothorax develops sudden breathlessness and falling oxygen saturation. Previously visible respiratory swinging has stopped. What is the priority?
A. Record that the lung has re-expanded and request routine confirmation at the next review.
B. Assess breathing, summon urgent help and check for visible kinks or drainage-system problems.
C. Clamp the drain temporarily to see whether the patient's respiratory symptoms improve.
D. Flush the drain against resistance before calling the team to confirm that it is blocked.
**Answer: B.**
**Why correct:** An unexpected loss of swinging may reflect obstruction, kinking, displacement or lung re-expansion; it does not prove any one cause. New hypoxaemia and breathlessness require immediate clinical assessment and emergency escalation. Inspect the accessible tubing and system without delaying support; a trained team determines further investigation and any authorized intervention. Do not force-flush or routinely clamp a pneumothorax drain.
**Why not A:** Absent swinging alone cannot confirm re-expansion, and the new clinical deterioration takes priority over routine documentation.
**Why not C:** Clamping a pneumothorax drain can impede air escape and worsen pressure accumulation.
**Why not D:** Flushing against resistance is unsafe; investigation and any flushing require a trained operator and the applicable procedure.
**Trace:** N-BTS-PCS-2023 — Non-functioning drain assessment, observation of respiratory swinging and urgent review of clinical deterioration.

**Q5 (judgment).** A patient with a newly inserted pleural-fluid drain becomes more breathless. Drainage is proceeding according to the written plan, but no suction is prescribed. A colleague suggests connecting suction to improve symptoms. What should the nurse do?
A. Assess the deterioration and seek urgent pleural review rather than initiating unprescribed suction.
B. Connect low suction while assessing the patient, then confirm the pressure with the team.
C. Increase drainage by adding suction and ask the team to review the response at the next round.
D. Wait until the patient is comfortable before reporting the new symptoms or changing the drainage plan.
**Answer: A.**
**Why correct:** New breathlessness requires immediate assessment and escalation, including consideration of drainage-related complications. BTS advises avoiding suction shortly after insertion to reduce re-expansion pulmonary oedema risk and requires suction pressure to be specified if suction is indicated. Follow the written drainage instructions and authorized emergency pathway; the presence of symptoms is not an indication to improvise suction.
**Why not B:** Even low suction is not an authorized treatment and can add risk shortly after drain insertion.
**Why not C:** Increasing drainage without assessing deterioration may aggravate a drainage-related complication.
**Why not D:** Deferring assessment of new breathlessness can delay necessary emergency treatment.
**Trace:** N-BTS-PCS-2023 — Early suction avoidance, prescribed pressure and assessment of new respiratory symptoms.

**Q6 (monitoring).** At the start of the shift the nurse finds that the patient's conventional underwater-seal drain bottle has a water level below the device's specified minimum. What is the correct nursing action?
A. Ignore it, because the water level matters mainly while the patient is breathing out strongly.
B. Top it up with any available sterile water without checking the system, as the level looks low.
C. Raise the bottle above the patient so the little water left covers the tube end.
D. Assess, escalate and restore the specified seal under the trained procedure.
**Answer: D.**
**Why correct:** A conventional water seal must function at the specified level. Assess breathing and circulation first and arrange urgent trained assistance if the patient deteriorates. Follow the device manual and aseptic local procedure to restore the seal; these instructions do not apply to dry-seal or digital systems.
**Why not A:** The water seal matters whenever the drain is in use.
**Why not B:** Handling the system follows an aseptic procedure and local instruction.
**Why not C:** Raising the bottle is unsafe; the bottle stays below the insertion site.
**Trace:** N-BTS-PCS-2023 — Adequate water in system to cover end of tube; ANTT when changing bottle/tubing.
