# Central venous catheter care (CDC 2011)
> DRAFT – NOT clinically reviewed. AI-generated. Unpublished; never imported into the question bank.

## 17. MCQs
**Q1 (application).** You find the transparent dressing over a patient's central line lifting at one edge after he has been sweating. What is the right action?
A. Press the edge back down and review it at the next scheduled dressing change.
B. Add a second dressing over the first one to keep the edge down until the next change.
C. Replace the dressing using the unit's sterile technique now.
D. Tape the lifted edge with clean tape so the original dressing stays in place for its full interval.
**Answer: C.**
**Why correct:** CDC advises replacing a catheter site dressing that becomes damp, loosened or visibly soiled (Category IB).
**Why not A:** A loosened dressing no longer protects the site.
**Why not B:** Layering over a compromised dressing does not restore a sterile cover.
**Why not D:** Tape does not make a loosened dressing sterile again.
**Trace:** S-CDC-BSI-2011 — Replace dressing if damp, loosened or visibly soiled (IB).

**Q2 (safety).** A colleague is about to give an intermittent injection through the needleless connector of a central line. What must happen first?
A. Nothing extra is needed because the connector was disinfected at the start of the shift.
B. Disinfect the connector before access, following the approved antiseptic procedure.
C. Wipe the connector only if it looks visibly dirty, since clean connectors carry little risk.
D. Flush the connector with sterile water first, which removes any organisms from the outside.
**Answer: B.**
**Why correct:** CDC (Category IA) and the SHEA 2022 essential practices call for disinfecting hubs, needleless connectors and ports before every access. Follow the antiseptic manufacturer's contact and drying instructions and local policy.
**Why not A:** Disinfection is required before every access.
**Why not C:** The practice applies every time, not only when the connector looks dirty.
**Why not D:** Flushing does not disinfect the outside surface of the connector.
**Trace:** S-CDC-BSI-2011, S-SHEA-CLABSI-2022 — Disinfect hubs, connectors and ports before every access (IA).

**Q3 (judgment).** A nurse wants to put antibiotic ointment on a central line insertion site 'to be extra safe' for a patient who is not on dialysis. What should you tell them?
A. CDC advises against topical antibiotic ointments or creams at insertion sites, except for dialysis catheters.
B. It is a good idea, because ointment adds a barrier that lowers the chance of bacteria reaching the skin.
C. It is acceptable if a thin film is used and the site is cleaned at every dressing change.
D. It is acceptable for patients with fever, because fever suggests the site may be colonised.
**Answer: A.**
**Why correct:** CDC says not to use topical antibiotic ointment or creams on insertion sites except for dialysis catheters, because they may promote fungal infections and resistance.
**Why not B:** This is the practice the guideline advises against.
**Why not C:** The thickness of the film does not change the recommendation.
**Why not D:** Fever calls for assessment and escalation, not ointment.
**Trace:** S-CDC-BSI-2011 — Do not use topical antibiotic ointment on insertion sites except dialysis catheters (IB).

**Q4 (application).** A patient with a central line asks to shower. Which advice fits the CDC guideline?
A. Water contact is not allowed for as long as the line is in place, so offer a bed bath.
B. Showering is fine without cover if the dressing is a transparent one and the shower is short.
C. A bath is fine as long as the line site stays above the water level for most of the time.
D. Showering can be allowed if the catheter and connecting device are protected with an impermeable cover, but the site must not be submerged.
**Answer: D.**
**Why correct:** CDC says not to submerge the catheter or site in water, and that showering is permitted if precautions reduce the chance of introducing organisms, such as an impermeable cover.
**Why not A:** The guideline allows showering with precautions.
**Why not B:** The catheter and connecting device still need protection.
**Why not C:** Submerging the catheter or site is not advised.
**Trace:** S-CDC-BSI-2011 — Do not submerge catheter/site; showering permitted with protection (IB).

**Q5 (monitoring).** An adult patient has a short-term central line with a transparent dressing labelled eight days ago that still looks intact. How should the nurse act?
A. Leave it, because an intact dressing does not need changing until it becomes damp or soiled.
B. Change it if the patient develops a fever, because fever is the signal to look at the site.
C. Arrange the due dressing change under the unit's approved short-term CVC dressing procedure.
D. Replace the label and the tape so the dressing stays in place and the date looks current.
**Answer: C.**
**Why correct:** The CDC recommendation is to change transparent dressings on short-term CVC sites at least every seven days, with a specific paediatric exception. For this adult, the labelled eight-day interval is overdue; follow the approved dressing procedure and replace earlier when the dressing is compromised.
**Why not A:** An interval applies even when the dressing looks intact.
**Why not B:** Waiting for fever delays a scheduled change.
**Why not D:** Relabelling does not renew a dressing that has passed its interval.
**Trace:** S-CDC-BSI-2011 — Transparent dressings on short-term CVC sites replaced at least every 7 days (IB); follow unit interval.

**Q6 (scope).** During a multidisciplinary round, a clinically stable patient has a central catheter that has not been used for two days. The nurse is reviewing whether there is still a clinical indication for the line. What is the best contribution to the decision?
A. Leave it in place as a precaution, because a line that is already in carries no extra risk.
B. Ask the team whether the line is still needed.
C. Remove it yourself immediately, since you are the person who looks after the line every day.
D. Change the dressing and flush the line, because this shows the line is working and may stay.
**Answer: B.**
**Why correct:** Review ongoing need and arrange removal when the line is no longer essential. WHO 2026 recommends daily assessment of the site and suggests against routine central catheter replacement without a clinical indication. Removal is performed by a credentialed clinician under the authorised local pathway.
**Why not A:** Every line day carries infection risk.
**Why not C:** Removal needs an order or an approved protocol.
**Why not D:** A patent catheter and an intact dressing do not establish an ongoing indication. Maintenance is required while the catheter remains necessary, but the team should separately assess whether removal is appropriate.
**Trace:** S-CDC-BSI-2011, S-SHEA-CLABSI-2022, S-WHO-CVC-2026 — Review necessity, daily site assessment and no routine CVC replacement without clinical indication.
