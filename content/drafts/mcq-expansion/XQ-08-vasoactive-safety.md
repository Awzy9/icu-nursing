# Vasoactive medication safety (FDA labels)

## 17. MCQs
**Q1 (safety).** A patient's norepinephrine is infusing through a peripheral line when the skin over the cannula becomes pale, cool and swollen. What should you do?
A. Slow the infusion and recheck in an hour, because the change may be a reaction to the cold drug.
B. Stop delivery through that cannula now, summon help, and activate the unit's extravasation pathway.
C. Massage the area to disperse the drug, since this spreads it over a wider area and reduces harm.
D. Apply a cold pack for comfort and continue the infusion until the next scheduled site check.
**Answer: B.**
**Why correct:** Norepinephrine extravasation can cause tissue ischemia and necrosis. Stop delivery through the affected cannula immediately, assess the site and patient, summon trained help, and arrange continuing vasopressor support through safe access. Whether to retain/aspirate the cannula or administer an antidote is controlled by the device/unit extravasation protocol; do not force-flush a suspected extravasation.
**Why not A:** Delay risks tissue necrosis.
**Why not C:** Massage is not advised and may worsen tissue injury.
**Why not D:** Continuing an infusion through a suspected extravasation worsens the injury.
**Trace:** L-NOREPINEPHRINE — Label: tissue ischaemia; avoid extravasation; local necrosis.

**Q2 (judgment).** The line carrying a patient's norepinephrine becomes occluded and the pump alarms. The patient's blood pressure is falling. What is the best action?
A. Assess, summon help and restore the prescribed infusion via patent access per protocol.
B. Wait for the pump to clear itself, because the alarm will stop once the pressure equalises.
C. Disconnect the infusion and flush the line hard to remove the blockage.
D. Give a fluid bolus yourself as soon as the alarm sounds and then deal with the line.
**Answer: A.**
**Why correct:** The norepinephrine label warns abrupt interruption can cause marked hypotension. The immediate priority is clinical assessment, urgent help and safe restoration of the ordered infusion through confirmed patent access; never force-flush an occluded vasoactive line or improvise a bolus.
**Why not B:** An occluded line will not clear by waiting, and the interruption is dangerous.
**Why not C:** A forceful flush may deliver a bolus and is not an approved response.
**Why not D:** Fluid is a prescriber or protocol decision; the interruption needs urgent action and escalation.
**Trace:** L-NOREPINEPHRINE — Label: hypotension after abrupt discontinuation.

**Q3 (monitoring).** Twelve hours after vasopressin is stopped, a patient's urine output rises sharply and the urine looks pale and watery, and the sodium is climbing. What should you suspect and do?
A. Reassure the team that this is the diuretic phase of recovery and record the output.
B. Restart the vasopressin yourself to see whether the urine output settles.
C. Restrict all fluids until the morning blood tests, to protect the sodium level.
D. Report possible reversible diabetes insipidus and monitor urine output, fluid status and electrolytes as the label advises.
**Answer: D.**
**Why correct:** The label describes reversible diabetes insipidus (polyuria, dilute urine, hypernatraemia) after stopping vasopressin and advises monitoring serum electrolytes, fluid status and urine output.
**Why not A:** The label names a specific cause that needs reporting.
**Why not B:** Restarting is a prescriber decision.
**Why not C:** Fluid management is a prescriber decision.
**Trace:** L-VASOPRESSIN — Label: reversible diabetes insipidus after cessation; monitor electrolytes, fluid status, urine output.

**Q4 (monitoring).** A patient with atrial fibrillation on dobutamine has a sudden rise in heart rate to a rapid, irregular rhythm. What does the label's warning suggest?
A. Treat it as a normal effect of the drug and record the rate at the next hourly check.
B. Stop the infusion permanently and record that the patient is intolerant of the drug.
C. Assess the patient and perfusion immediately, report possible rapid ventricular response and summon emergency help if unstable.
D. Give extra fluid yourself, because a fast heart rate always means the patient is dry.
**Answer: C.**
**Why correct:** The label warns that because dobutamine facilitates atrioventricular conduction, patients with atrial fibrillation are at risk of a rapid ventricular response. Assess blood pressure, perfusion and symptoms alongside the ECG; activate the emergency pathway for clinical instability.
**Why not A:** A marked rise needs prompt reporting.
**Why not B:** Changing the infusion is a prescriber or protocol decision.
**Why not D:** Fluid is a prescriber decision and the cause here may be the drug.
**Trace:** L-DOBUTAMINE — Label: AF patients at risk of rapid ventricular response.

**Q5 (monitoring).** A patient on milrinone has more ventricular ectopic beats appearing on the monitor over an hour. What does the label support?
A. Silence the alarm limits so the ectopics stop triggering the monitor and wait for the ward round.
B. Report promptly, because the label links milrinone with more ventricular arrhythmias and calls for continuous ECG monitoring.
C. Do nothing because ectopic beats are harmless in patients with heart failure.
D. Stop the continuous ECG because it is causing the alarms, and take a pulse each hour instead.
**Answer: B.**
**Why correct:** The milrinone label reports ventricular arrhythmias including nonsustained ventricular tachycardia and calls for close observation with continuous ECG monitoring. The new ectopy also requires patient assessment and urgent escalation; it should not be assumed to be caused solely by the drug.
**Why not A:** Widening alarm limits hides the problem.
**Why not C:** The label treats ventricular arrhythmia as an important risk.
**Why not D:** Continuous ECG monitoring is the label's expectation.
**Trace:** L-MILRINONE — Label: ventricular arrhythmias; continuous ECG monitoring.

**Q6 (application).** A patient has anaphylaxis and the team authorizes an *intramuscular* epinephrine injection using an appropriate product. Which site does the cited intramuscular product label identify?
A. The anterolateral thigh, avoiding the buttocks, digits, hands and feet.
B. The deltoid, because the arm is the quickest site to reach in a patient who is lying down.
C. The buttock, because the muscle is large and has a good blood supply.
D. The fingertip, which gives the fastest absorption into the bloodstream in an emergency.
**Answer: A.**
**Why correct:** The route-specific Adrenalin anaphylaxis label identifies the anterolateral thigh (vastus lateralis) for intramuscular injection. It warns against injection into the buttocks, digits, hands or feet. This does **not** describe the intravenous epinephrine infusion used for septic-shock hypotension; verify the product and ordered route.
**Why not B:** The intramuscular anaphylaxis label discourages injection into smaller muscles such as the deltoid.
**Why not C:** The label says not to inject into the buttocks.
**Why not D:** The label says not to inject into digits.
**Trace:** S-ADRENALIN-IM-2025 — Adrenalin anaphylaxis IM labeling, sections 2.2 and 5.1. L-EPINEPHRINE separately concerns IV septic-shock use and must not be substituted as evidence for an IM injection site.
