## 17. MCQs
**Q1 (safety).** A patient's norepinephrine is infusing through a peripheral line when the skin over the cannula becomes pale, cool and swollen. What should you do?
A. Slow the infusion and recheck in an hour, because the change may be a reaction to the cold drug.
B. Stop delivery through that cannula now, summon help, and activate the unit's extravasation pathway.
C. Massage the area to disperse the drug, since this spreads it over a wider area and reduces harm.
D. Apply a cold pack for comfort and continue the infusion until the next scheduled site check.
**Answer: B.**
**Why correct:** Suspected norepinephrine extravasation threatens tissue perfusion. Stop delivery at the affected site, obtain urgent help and arrange safe alternative access to maintain necessary circulatory support. Preserve the catheter for the trained team's extravasation assessment and do not force-flush a suspected extravasation. SSC 2026 conditionally favors peripheral initiation in adult septic shock when waiting for central access would delay vasopressor support, but does not establish a universal dose, duration or cannula size/site.
**Why not A:** Delay risks tissue necrosis.
**Why not C:** Massage is not advised and may worsen tissue injury.
**Why not D:** Continuing an infusion through a suspected extravasation worsens the injury.
**Trace:** L-NOREPINEPHRINE, S-SSC-2026 — Extravasation precautions and conditional peripheral initiation with insufficient evidence for universal access parameters.

**Q2 (judgment).** The line carrying a patient's norepinephrine becomes occluded and the pump alarms. The patient's blood pressure is falling. What is the best action?
A. Assess, summon help and restore the prescribed infusion via patent access per protocol.
B. Wait for the pump to clear itself, because the alarm will stop once the pressure equalises.
C. Disconnect the infusion and flush the line hard to remove the blockage.
D. Give a fluid bolus yourself as soon as the alarm sounds and then deal with the line.
**Answer: A.**
**Why correct:** Abrupt interruption of norepinephrine can cause severe hypotension. Assess and summon urgent help; verify line identity and restore the prescribed infusion through confirmed patent access under the backup plan. Do not flush residual vasoactive drug into the patient, bypass the pump or improvise a bolus.
**Why not B:** An occluded line will not clear by waiting, and the interruption is dangerous.
**Why not C:** A forceful flush may deliver a bolus and is not an approved response.
**Why not D:** Fluid is a prescriber or protocol decision; the interruption needs urgent action and escalation.
**Trace:** L-NOREPINEPHRINE — Label: hypotension after abrupt discontinuation.

**Q3 (judgment).** After vasopressin is discontinued, a recovering shock patient develops a marked rise in dilute urine output, increasing sodium and a falling fluid balance. The nurse considers whether this is simply recovery from earlier fluid accumulation. What is the best action?
A. Document recovery diuresis and continue the routine fluid assessment without discussing the change.
B. Restart the previous vasopressin infusion and use the urine response to establish the diagnosis.
C. Restrict fluid intake until the next sodium result confirms that the change is persistent.
D. Escalate possible transient diabetes insipidus and assess fluid and electrolyte trends.
**Answer: D.**
**Why correct:** Vasopressin prescribing information describes reversible diabetes insipidus after cessation, with polyuria, dilute urine and hypernatraemia. The pattern warrants prompt assessment of circulation, intake, output, sodium and other relevant findings, with clinician-directed investigation and fluid management. Other causes of polyuria remain possible; the temporal association does not establish a diagnosis or authorize restarting the infusion.
**Why not A:** Recovery diuresis is one possibility, but rising sodium and negative balance warrant prompt reassessment rather than reassurance.
**Why not B:** A trial restart could cause harm and is not an independent diagnostic or nursing treatment decision.
**Why not C:** Fluid restriction may worsen water deficit; the appropriate fluid plan requires individualized clinical assessment.
**Trace:** L-VASOPRESSIN — Reversible diabetes insipidus following cessation; monitor serum electrolytes, fluid status and urine output.

**Q4 (monitoring).** A patient with atrial fibrillation on dobutamine has a sudden rise in heart rate to a rapid, irregular rhythm. What does the label's warning suggest?
A. Treat it as a normal effect of the drug and record the rate at the next hourly check.
B. Stop the infusion permanently and record that the patient is intolerant of the drug.
C. Assess perfusion and escalate the possible rapid ventricular response now.
D. Give extra fluid yourself, because a fast heart rate always means the patient is dry.
**Answer: C.**
**Why correct:** The label warns that because dobutamine facilitates atrioventricular conduction, patients with atrial fibrillation are at risk of a rapid ventricular response. Assess blood pressure, perfusion and symptoms alongside the ECG; activate the emergency pathway for clinical instability.
**Why not A:** A marked rise needs prompt reporting.
**Why not B:** Changing the infusion is a prescriber or protocol decision.
**Why not D:** Fluid is a prescriber decision and the cause here may be the drug.
**Trace:** L-DOBUTAMINE — Label: AF patients at risk of rapid ventricular response.

**Q5 (judgment).** A patient receiving milrinone develops worsening kidney function, falling blood pressure and new ventricular ectopy. The infusion has not changed since the previous day. What should the nurse prioritize?
A. Wait for the next routine renal result because the unchanged infusion makes an adverse drug effect unlikely.
B. Assess perfusion and rhythm, escalate urgently and request review of the renal dosing plan.
C. Increase milrinone to improve renal blood flow while the patient is still producing urine.
D. Give extra fluid for the falling pressure before reporting the new rhythm and renal changes.
**Answer: B.**
**Why correct:** Milrinone can cause ventricular arrhythmias and hypotension; renal impairment prolongs its elimination and may require an adjusted infusion plan. Assess the patient, maintain continuous ECG and blood pressure surveillance, review renal and electrolyte trends and escalate urgently. A constant infusion rate does not establish constant exposure when clearance changes. The prescribing team determines dose adjustment; other causes of deterioration must also be evaluated.
**Why not A:** Worsening kidney function can change drug exposure even when the pump setting is unchanged.
**Why not C:** Increasing the infusion without review may worsen hypotension or arrhythmia and ignores the change in renal clearance.
**Why not D:** Additional fluid is not automatically indicated by hypotension; assess the patient and follow the authorized haemodynamic plan.
**Trace:** L-MILRINONE — Ventricular arrhythmias, continuous ECG surveillance, hypotension and prolonged elimination with renal impairment.

**Q6 (application).** A patient has anaphylaxis and the team authorizes an *intramuscular* epinephrine injection using an appropriate product. Which site does the cited intramuscular product label identify?
A. The anterolateral thigh, avoiding the buttocks, digits, hands and feet.
B. The deltoid, because the arm is the quickest site to reach in a patient who is lying down.
C. The buttock, because the muscle is large and has a good blood supply.
D. The fingertip, which gives the fastest absorption into the bloodstream in an emergency.
**Answer: A.**
**Why correct:** The cited IM product identifies the anterolateral thigh. Prompt IM adrenaline is first-line treatment for anaphylaxis with airway, breathing or circulation compromise. Do not delay emergency treatment for routine prescribing steps; reassess promptly and use the authorized repeat-treatment pathway if compromise persists. IV adrenaline for spontaneous circulation requires specialist management; verify the product and route.
**Why not B:** The intramuscular anaphylaxis label discourages injection into smaller muscles such as the deltoid.
**Why not C:** The label says not to inject into the buttocks.
**Why not D:** The label says not to inject into digits.
**Trace:** S-ADRENALIN-IM-2025, N-RCUK-ANAPH-2021 — Route-specific injection site and time-critical emergency treatment; IV septic-shock labeling must not be substituted as evidence for an IM injection site.
