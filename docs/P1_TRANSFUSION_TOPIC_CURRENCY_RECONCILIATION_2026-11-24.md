## Source checks and proposed changes

- [BSH acute transfusion reactions, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjh.18789): publisher HTML now returned the recommendation summary and immediate management sections (previous retrieval was blocked). Selected passages, not the entire guideline or appendices, were checked. The initial stop/assess/escalate response and authorized mild-reaction restart in sections 7 and 12 are consistent. BSH distinguishes hypotension from ongoing haemorrhage from suspected component-related hypotension (1C). Add a specific warning in section 12: during active major bleeding, urgent senior review must distinguish ongoing blood loss from a possible transfusion reaction; do not delay lifesaving resuscitation while awaiting investigations. Do not independently continue or restart a suspect component. BSH also specifies IM adrenaline for anaphylaxis (1A); clarify route within the authorized emergency pathway, without adding dosing. Review the linked scenario SC-09 and SC-14 for consistency before implementation. BSH recommendations and rights remain only partially appraised.

Add to section 9 that overload may first manifest up to 12 hours after transfusion; TRALI generally begins during or within six hours. These are recognition windows, not a reason to ignore later deterioration. Canadian guidance describes TRALI type II despite ARDS risk factors or stable mild ARDS; the current wording could incorrectly exclude such patients. Distinguish the two syndromes without treating blood pressure, fluid balance or biomarkers as diagnostic alone. Do not teach empiric diuresis for undifferentiated respiratory deterioration. Preserve prompt cessation, ABC assessment and expert escalation. No doses or copied tables.
