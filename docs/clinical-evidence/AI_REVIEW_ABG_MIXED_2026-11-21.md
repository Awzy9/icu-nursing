## Findings and edits

| Finding | Why it matters | Corrected teaching | Supporting source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simplified claim: "fully compensated means pH has returned to normal" | Normal-range pH can coexist with **two opposing primary acid–base disorders**. Compensation of one simple disturbance does not reliably explain all values or necessarily normalize pH | Replaced the shortcut with a warning that pH alone cannot certify complete compensation; compare expected physiological response and investigate metabolic/respiratory components | American Thoracic Society (ATS) ABG teaching, steps 2–5; Merck Manual Professional, Diagnosis |
| No mixed-disorder example for bedside escalation | Learners could wrongly defer a dangerous bicarbonate/pCO₂ abnormality when pH is 7.40 | Added internally consistent illustrative ABG pH 7.40, PaCO₂ 20 mm Hg, HCO₃ 12 mEq/L; ATS metabolic-acidosis compensation predicts PaCO₂ about 26 ± 2. Lower observed PaCO₂ suggests additional primary respiratory alkalosis | ATS, compensation and mixed-disorder section; Merck Manual Professional |
| Case called metabolic alkalosis "uncompensated" based solely on normal laboratory-range PaCO₂ | Reference interval membership **does not prove** absent physiological compensation | Removed "uncompensated" and required assessment of expected compensation and clinical context | ATS; Merck |
| One MCQ taught a limited single-disorder pattern without testing the dangerous failure mode | Misleading reassurance is a more serious educational risk than missing a basic respiratory-alkalosis drill | Replaced Q4 with a normal-pH mixed-disorder safety vignette; **six MCQs retained**, answer and explanations checked | ATS; Merck |
| 2013 AARC statement about venous blood sampling could be interpreted as universal clinical prohibition | VBG pH/bicarbonate can aid assessment in selected scenarios, but venous pO₂ **cannot** determine arterial oxygenation; venous pCO₂ is imperfect in unstable patients | Preserved the **2013 AARC wording as its dated recommendation** and added an explicitly limited recent-practice context; not a substitution algorithm | AAFP Feb 2025 public summary/approach, Merck Professional and AARC 2013 |
