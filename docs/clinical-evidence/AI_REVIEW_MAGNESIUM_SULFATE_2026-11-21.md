## Findings and corrections

| Gap or risk | Label evidence | Education corrected |
|---|---|---|
| Adverse-effects section omitted respiratory paralysis and cardiac/CNS depression | Label describes depressed or absent tendon reflexes, CNS/neuromuscular weakness, severe hypotension, circulatory collapse and respiratory paralysis in significant magnesium intoxication | Expanded into organ-specific harms and urgent bedside escalation for respiratory or circulatory compromise |
| Interactions missing | Label warns of additive CNS depression with sedatives, narcotics/anesthetics and excessive neuromuscular blockade with paralytics; special caution with cardiac glycosides if calcium rescue is needed | Added medication reconciliation and clinician/pharmacy oversight; no independent dose changes |
| Label-specific contraindications missing | Hospira product lists heart block or myocardial damage among contraindications | Add product-specific screening/clinician confirmation rather than inventing a universal rule across preparations |
| Concentrated formulation risk | Product package says concentration-specific syringe requires preparation before IV use | Clarify concentrate syringe vs ready-to-use bag; **no concentrations, volumes, rates or preparation instructions** reproduced |
| Renal impairment and delayed events under-specified | Magnesium is renally excreted; impaired function raises accumulation risk. The specific product also warns of aluminum exposure in vulnerable populations; maternal/neonatal effects are context-dependent | Add renal/urine output/serum magnesium review and appropriately limited vulnerable-population flags |
| Inadequate surveillance language | Label ties monitoring to serum magnesium and clinical status, especially reflexes, breathing and blood pressure | Add symptom-focused monitoring and emergency escalation without turning this lesson into treatment orders |

- Pharmacy must own dose, concentration, dilution, route compatibility and administration standards.
