## D02-T08 — shock recognition: deeper evidence inspection, corrective specification

**Source read in this turn:** ESICM 2025 shock guideline version-of-record full-text **opening, introduction, abstract and Table 1 comparing 2014/2025**, via institutional repository PDF text. The entire 42-page publication, evidence tables and supplements were **not** read and must not be marked full-read. DOI: 10.1007/s00134-025-08137-z.

**Confirmed inconsistency:** the draft's `## 20` text says *"No newer open-access shock consensus was found"*; the 2025 guideline explicitly **updates the 2014 consensus** and can be accessed via ESICM and a research repository. This sentence is false and should be removed in the clinical-draft revision, with traceable source-register metadata.

**Clinical comparisons from the primary 2025 document's Table 1:**
- Shock is framed around **decreased tissue perfusion**; assessment should be serial, including **capillary refill time**, skin temperature and mottling as complementary signs.
- Shock has different hemodynamic profiles. The older generic 2014 starting **MAP ≥65** should not be represented as a universal current target for all shock types: the newer comparison distinguishes septic shock, hemorrhage with/without brain injury, and cardiogenic shock. Prescribers set targets.
- In persistent shock after initial resuscitation, 2025 calls for assessing fluid responsiveness before additional fluid, using appropriate **dynamic indicators over static preload markers**.
- Echocardiography remains the first-line imaging option to assess shock type; invasive monitoring is discussed for persistent/nonresponsive cases and vasopressor infusion.

**Required editorial change:** register ESICM 2025 as a distinct cite-only reference, preserve the 2014 source as historical, reconcile the MCQs and hemodynamic values with the 2025 guideline's exact recommendations and strength. **Do not** invent review/approval status. Clinical draft was not edited this turn because its source IDs and licensing register need a consistent update before release of an evidence-traceable claim.

## D07-T04 — pressure injury prevention: full chapter now accessible

**Read in this turn:** Official International Guideline 2026 **Repositioning and Mobilization** web chapter, relevant sections (not the entire chapter bibliography), and official **Repositioning Part Two** section on proning. Chapter available in full; cannot claim every chapter/evidence table has been read.

**Important incremental finding:** the full guideline includes a **conditional, very-low-certainty** option of 2-hourly **or** 3-hourly repositioning for many people at risk *when using an appropriate pressure-redistribution surface*, with explicit individualization. The draft currently teaches individualized schedules rather than a compulsory fixed 2-hour frequency, which **does not contradict** this statement. Critically ill patients with hypoperfusion may need smaller, more frequent positioning changes. It also discusses prone positioning when required for the underlying condition; pressure-injury prevention does not override necessary respiratory care.

**Required editorial work:** cross-check the device-related pressure injury and heel/skin-assessment full chapters, not just QRG; update the source-depth notes and copyright licensing after a complete applicable-chapter read. **Do not** infer that the full guideline's general repositioning interval supersedes clinician judgment or the unit's safety protocols.
